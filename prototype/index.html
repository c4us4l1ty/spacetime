The Dark Sky Paradox

FIELD NOTES · 15 NOV · 04:12 UTC · AUTOSAVED TO OVERLAY

If the universe is infinite and ageless, every line of sight should eventually end on the surface of a star. The night sky should be as bright as the surface of the Sun. It is not.

Olbers' paradox is not a paradox about darkness. It is a paradox about the word eventually. Light has a speed, and the universe has a beginning, so "eventually" never gets to finish its sentence. The sky is dark because it is young.

"The darkness is not the absence of stars. It is the presence of time."

What we call the night sky is a record of how far light has come, and how much of that journey is still in transit. Every photon is a fossil with a departure date, and the darkest parts of the sky are the parts still waiting for their mail.

The same is true of a workspace. A tab is not just a window; it is a slice of time that has not yet been overwritten. Freeze it, and the light stops arriving. Wake it, and the delay is only ever as long as the distance you put between the two states.
